MARKETING COPY AND SOCIAL CRAFT

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading three marketing skills from a larger MIT pack the owner uploaded (copywriting, social content, launch strategy); the originals and the licence are kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/marketing/. Use it when the person wants words that sell what they make: a landing page, a headline, a caption, a social post or thread, or a launch plan.

1. DIAGNOSE BEFORE WRITING
- Blank or generic: build the argument from the brief; you may choose the angle, never the facts. No invented statistics, customers, results or testimonials; mark gaps as [NEED: ...] and say what evidence would fill them.
- A real voice is already there: keep their argument and their words ("stuff", "ship"), fix the buried point, vague lines and structure. Their angle is the positioning.
- A point edit ("fix this headline"): change only that, plus the least needed around it. Hand the rest back untouched.
- Say the diagnosis in one line with the evidence before you draft.

2. WHAT TO KNOW (ask only what is missing)
The one action the page or post should lead to; who it is for, what problem they are in and the words they use for it; what is offered, what makes it different and the outcome; any proof that is real; and where the reader comes from and what they already know.

3. THE THREE-QUESTION GATE ON EVERY LINE
Can the reader picture it? Can it be proven true or false? Could a competitor write this exact sentence? A line that fails the third (the swap test) is rewritten from the facts. Draft two or three versions of each unit (headline, subhead, button, each section) and keep the best. Spend the punch: most lines plain and specific, one or two moments that land.

4. PRINCIPLES
Clear beats clever. Benefits over features. Specific over vague ("cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes", not "save time"). Customer words over company words. One idea per section. Simple words, active voice, confident (drop "very", "really", "almost"), outcomes instead of adverbs, no exclamation marks, no buzzwords without substance. Only claims the source supports: when a requested claim is not supported, name it, say why, offer the closest supportable line and say what evidence would unlock the original.
Avoid the machine tells: stacked triplets of short sentences, "it's not X, it's Y", filler openers, em dashes, and words like unlock, leverage, seamless, game-changing.

5. SOCIAL POSTS
- The story comes first; platform tactics only amplify it. Pick three to five pillars the person can keep up (for example what they know, how they build, how-tos, personal, and a small share of promotion).
- The first line decides everything. Hook shapes: a curiosity ("I was wrong about..."), a story ("last week something unexpected happened"), a value promise ("how to [result] without [pain]"), a contrarian line. Then deliver the promise, end with one clear next step.
- Reuse one idea across formats (a clip, a carousel, a short post) rather than writing fresh for each platform. For clips, move to the short-form and Cinema notes.

6. LAUNCHES
Build momentum in stages: tease and collect interest, a clear announcement with one core message, then follow-ups that show real use and results. Pick a few channels you can actually serve well rather than all of them. Prepare the facts, the visuals, the reply plan and the follow-up before the day. Match the size of the launch to the size of the news.

7. CHECK BEFORE HANDING OVER
Is every changed line measurably better or only different? Revert changes you cannot give a reason for. Does anything claim more than the facts show?

8. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Atlas)
- Give the line or the draft first, with at most two lines on what you assumed. Offer the next piece instead of writing everything.
