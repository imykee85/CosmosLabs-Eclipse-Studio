SHORT-FORM AND FILM CRAFT

Eclipse notes written from three skill packs the owner uploaded (a filmmaking guide, an anti-"AI look" filmmaking pack and a short-form writing pack). The originals are kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/filmmaking-short-form/. This is for planning and writing videos and their prompts. Use it when the person is making a short video, a script, a hook or a film, and keep it quiet otherwise.

1. THE FIRST SECONDS
- A hook works when it is clear (the viewer knows what this is about) and curious (they want the next line). Clarity first, curiosity second.
- Four hook killers: a slow warm-up, a vague promise, a long first sentence (aim for 22 words or fewer), and starting with who you are.
- Formula: a number or a named thing, a contrast or a surprise, then a reason to stay. Two or three lines is enough.
- The first frame must already show something worth looking at. Test: show someone the first 3 seconds and ask what the video is about; if they cannot say, the hook is not clear.

2. STRUCTURE FOR A SHORT VIDEO
- Seven parts: hook, promise, proof, the main beats, a turn, the payoff, a clear next step.
- Re-hook roughly every 5 to 8 seconds: a number, a twist, a reveal, or "first, second, finally".
- Pace of speech: about 3.6 to 4.6 words a second. A 30 second script is about 100 to 140 words; a 60 second one 200 to 230.
- Seven story beats when there is a story: situation, want, obstacle, attempt, turn, result, change.
- For a one-minute piece use Hook, Heart, Help, Hero: the grabber, the human reason, the useful idea, the payoff. For a longer piece plan five chapters, each with its own small hook.
- A loop helps: let the last line flow back into the first.

3. WRITING THAT DOES NOT SOUND MACHINE-MADE
Write for the ear. Read every line aloud; it must sound like talking.
- No em dashes, no semicolons in spoken lines.
- Avoid the patterns: "not just X but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "here's the thing", "let that sink in", "imagine this", "buckle up", "the truth is", "at the end of the day", "whether you're a...".
- Avoid fake suspense ("plot twist:", "here's where it gets interesting"), metaphor punchlines, and more than one one-word dramatic sentence.
- Avoid the word pile: delve, unlock, unleash, harness, leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionary, transformative, landscape, realm, tapestry, elevate, empower, supercharge, deep dive, next level.
- Only the hook asks a question; the body states things. Keep sentences under about 30 words. Explain any technical word the first time in ten words or fewer. Never claim "I tried this" unless it is true.

4. LOOK AND SOUND OF AN AI VIDEO THAT FEELS MADE, NOT GENERATED
- Prompts: describe optics and light, not praise. Instead of "photorealistic, 8k, masterpiece" write things like "shallow depth of field, 50mm, warm key light with a faint cool rim, soft volumetric haze". Do not use numeric ages (write "young adult") or named film stocks; describe the look instead.
- Every camera move has a reason: a push-in for intimacy, an orbit for scale, a crane reveal for a place, a macro tracking shot for detail. One move per shot.
- Cut to a new image about every 4 to 5 seconds on short vertical video. Vary shot size (wide, medium, close) so it does not feel like one shot repeated.
- Light is motivated: a key light with a reason and a rim to separate the subject. Flat, even light looks generated.
- Sound in layers: clear voice first; music underneath that ducks (drops about 18 dB) while someone speaks; small textures (paper, clicks, a soft low hit on a key reveal).
- Captions: word by word, bold clean sans-serif, white with one accent colour on the key word, bottom third, a soft shadow for legibility.

5. FINAL CHECKS BEFORE CALLING IT DONE
- Mute test: does it still make sense with the sound off?
- Eyes-closed test: does the audio alone hold you?
- Stranger test on the first 3 seconds.
- Hook, rhythm (a change every 4 to 5 s), lighting, voice clarity, and whether the last line loops to the first.
- After posting, read the numbers at 3 days: the point where the retention graph drops is the line to fix next time; under 1,000 views twice means drop that angle.

6. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Treatment and Atlas)
- Use these as judgement, not as a lecture. Give the person the one or two changes that matter most, not the whole list.
- When you write a script, keep it to what can be spoken in the planned length. When you write a prompt, keep it to one shot.
