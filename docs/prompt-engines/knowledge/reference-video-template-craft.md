REFERENCE VIDEO TO TEMPLATE

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading the owner's "analyze-video" skill (the original is kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/reference-video/). It turns a video the person likes into a reusable prompt template: a recipe they can fill with any product, person or place to get that style back. Use it when the person shares a video and says things like "make videos like this", "break this down", "turn this into a template" or "recreate this style". Atlas watches a clip as still frames with the watch tool and cannot hear it, so ask for the spoken lines when they matter.

1. LOOK FIRST
Watch the clip with enough frames to see every beat (more for a longer video, and look closer at any moment that matters). Note length, ratio and pace. If the sound matters (speech rhythm, filler words, how lines meet the action), ask the person for the lines, or say you can only judge the picture.

2. SEPARATE THE STYLE FROM THE SPECIFICS
For each thing you notice, ask: is this particular to THIS video (the person, the product, the room) or is it THE STYLE (the approach, the structure, the feel)? Only the style goes into the template; the specifics become fill-in slots.

3. WHAT TO LOOK AT
- Structure and pace: how many beats; which two or three define the style; the arc (hook, demo, proof, verdict, or something else); fast cuts or lingering shots; silent beats.
- Camera and framing: who is filming and how (selfie, propped phone, a second person, a screen recording); how the framing changes between beats; any signature move.
- Edit: jump cuts, one continuous take, time-lapse, split screen; how transitions work; repeating motifs (close-up product shots, reaction face, before and after).
- Words: the hook format (question, bold claim, mid-action, reaction); scripted or spoken off the cuff; sentence length and vocabulary; how lines relate to the action.
- Tone and energy: three or four feeling words; how energy changes; the relationship with the viewer (a friend, followers, thinking aloud).
- Light and quality: natural or ring light, polished or phone-made, which "flaws" are part of the look, how the sound was captured.
- Above all, what makes it different from a generic video of the same kind: the two or three traits that define it.

4. FIT IT TO ONE GENERATION
A single Seedance 2.5 clip runs 4 to 30 seconds, and the reference is often longer. Decide the smallest version that still feels like that style: two or three beats and two or three spoken lines for about 15 seconds. If the style's power is an arc or a list, plan two or three clips that are generated separately and joined; say which beat each clip carries and what must carry over between them (person, place, light, sound).

5. WRITE THE TEMPLATE (build only what this style needs; let the video's real structure decide the number of layers)
- A short "what defines this style" paragraph, so the person can make good choices rather than just fill blanks.
- The layers, in order. For each: what it does, the repeatable sentence pattern with {SLOTS}, and a small bank of options for each slot with a line of guidance.
- Rules that explain why certain choices matter, and what to avoid.
- Two complete examples, fully filled in, and the multi-clip plan if one is needed.
- Keep prompts to one moment and one camera move per clip, with attachments called "image 1", "video 1", "audio 1" in the order added.

6. RIGHTS
The template copies a style, not the original's people, voices, brands, music or footage. Say so, and never reproduce a recognisable person or a protected character.

7. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Atlas)
- Say in two lines what you saw and what defines the style, then give the template. Offer to turn it into a first prompt and to save it with the person's own notes. Do not claim to have heard what you could not.
