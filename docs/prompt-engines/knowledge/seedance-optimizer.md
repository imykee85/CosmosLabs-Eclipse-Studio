SEEDANCE 2.5 PROMPT OPTIMIZER

Eclipse notes written from the owner's "seedance25-prompt-optimizer" skill (the original, in Chinese, is kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/seedance-optimizer/). Translated and reworded for Eclipse: attachments are called image 1, image 2, video 1, audio 1 in the order the person added them (not @ tags). This is the quick path for turning a rough idea into one finished Seedance 2.5 prompt, and the structures for edits, extensions, keyframes and transitions. When the person wants the full guided storyboard process, use the director above instead.

WHEN TO USE THIS
A rough video idea to expand, a request for "a Seedance prompt", or any of: reference-based video, long video, editing a clip, extending a clip, first and last frame, several keyframes, a storyboard sheet, a blockout, a slideshow turned into a film, or a bridge between two clips.

WORKFLOW
1. Lock the hard constraints first: subject and how many, the event, scene, style, camera, sound, timing, the job of each attached file, exclusions, anything that must stay. Never overwrite them with defaults.
2. Pick ONE task type (table below). Use a second only when it is truly nested inside the first.
3. For an ordinary text-to-video idea, infer safe details instead of interrogating. Ask one short question only when a missing fact makes the task itself ambiguous (which object to replace, which clip comes first, what a reference picture is meant to define).
4. Add only detail that helps: action clarity, believable scene, camera that can be executed, fitting sound, continuity, reference binding. No decoration with no job.
5. Make abstract ideas observable (emotion, atmosphere, specialist camera terms).
6. Write natural sentences with clear state changes. For longer stories use stages, not dense timestamps.
7. Check: remove contradictions, repeated instructions, impossible precision, needless timestamps, and any accidental change to the number of people or the person's constraints.
8. Output the finished prompt only. Put capability warnings outside the prompt, in one line.
If the person asks what you changed, add a short list after the prompt. Never show your checklist.

TASK TYPES
Text to video: a rough idea with subject, action, scene. Reference-based: several attached pictures, clips or sounds, each with its own job. Long video: several events in sequence (about 20 to 30 s). Edit: change, replace, remove or add something in an existing clip. Extend: make an existing clip longer before or after. First and last frame: pictures used as the first and last frame. Several keyframes: ordered pictures that define states. Storyboard: a grid of panels. Coarse blockout: simple geometry gives the movement, positions and camera. Fine blockout: a complete structure exists, only the look is redone. Slideshow to film: many pictures become one short film. Seamless transition: a bridge between two clips.

THE BASE FORMULA
Subject and action or event, then scene and environment, then visual style, then camera or cuts, then sound. Skeleton: "[Subject] in [place] [main action or event]. The picture looks [style: light, colour, material, texture]. Shots: [size, angle, movement or cut]. Sound: [dialogue, ambience, effects, music]." Do not force every part when it adds nothing.
Order of authority: the person's explicit words, then what each attached file is for, then edit and continuity limits, then reliable inference, then low-risk defaults. A lower item never overrides a higher one.

SAFE TO ADD: where the subject starts, which way it faces, its posture and the visible end state; the natural path of the action; foreground, middle and background relationships; small environment motion that is physically consistent; light direction, shadows, reflections, wetness, material response; one simple shot size and move that serves the action; sounds that belong to the action; two to four observable signs of an emotion; continuity of identity, clothing, object count, who holds what, direction in space, camera axis and sound.
BE CAREFUL WITH: exact clothing colours, the country or era, strong colour grading, elaborate effects, extreme camera moves, music genre, weather, time of day.
NEVER INVENT: brands, logos, models or specifications; exact text, signs, formulas or interface copy; dialogue the person did not give; a real person's identity; plot twists; extra characters, animals, vehicles or key props; any change to the person's subject count, relationships, product structure or core scene.

VERY SHORT PROMPTS (no style given): natural realism with a restrained cinematic feel, few cuts, clear action, smooth camera, ambience and action sounds instead of invented dialogue, no subtitles, no dense timestamps, and a clear end state when the action would drift.

ACTION: write "start state, key action, visible result". Do not split a simple move into micro-actions. Weak: "she suddenly feels relieved." Better: "She stops what her hands were doing, lifts her eyes slowly from the phone, breathes out softly, her shoulders loosen and a restrained smile appears."
EMOTION: do not leave it as "tense" or "warm". Use two to four things a camera sees: eyes, brows, mouth, breathing, gaze, shoulder tension, hands, how they speak. Pattern: "The mood moves from [start] to [end]. After [trigger], [subject] first [immediate visible reaction]. Then [two to four changes]. Finally [action or expression] shows [target mood]."
CAMERA: common words can stand alone (wide, medium, close-up, push in, pull out, pan, tilt, tracking, orbit, handheld, low angle, top-down, first person). A specialist term (one take, dolly zoom, rack focus, whip pan, speed ramp, bullet time, FPV) also needs the subject, the starting relationship, direction and speed, what changes in front and behind, and the final state. Example: "Rack focus: focus moves smoothly from the foreground leaves to the person behind; the leaves blur and the face goes from soft to sharp." Prefer visible results over lens or shutter numbers. End on a clear hold.
SOUND: add only what belongs. Notation when it helps keep categories apart: music in ( ), effects in < >, dialogue in { }, on-screen text in 【 】. For speech that is not English, name the language and accent, the delivery, the speaker and give the exact words. "No music" means keep only the dialogue, ambience and effects asked for; "no sound" means invent none.
TIMING: stages for ordinary stories; timestamps only for entrances, exits, transitions and clearly timed beats. Ranges are continuous and never overlap; a range is a budget, not a frame-exact cut. One main event per interval.

STRUCTURES BY TASK
Reference-based. Give every file its own job, then map people and props one by one. "Image 1 gives [subject]'s [look, clothing, structure or material]; do not use its background or composition. Video 1 gives the [action, camera or rhythm]; do not use its person, clothing or place. Audio 1 gives [voice, lines, ambience or music]." Never write "images 1 to 4 define four characters"; bind each one. Several views of one subject: "Image 1 is the front, image 2 the left side, image 3 the right side; together they define one single subject, and only one of it appears." If a reference clip already fixes action, camera and order, say what to inherit instead of rewriting it.
Long video. "Goal: a [type] of [subject] [overall event]. Stage 1: starts [state]; main event [one action]; ends [visible state]. Stage 2: continues from [state to keep]; main event [one]; ends [state]. Stage 3: closing event; final picture. Keep [identity, count, clothing, who holds what, direction in space and sound] steady." One main state change per stage.
Edit. The source clip is the single master. "Edit video 1: in [the whole clip or a stated part], [add, remove, replace or adjust] [object, area or sound]. Video 1 is the only master and carries people, scene, action, composition, camera, occlusion, sound and the order of events. Image 1 or audio 1 gives [the target's look or sound]. Only change [object, area, time span or sound type]. Keep [what must not change]." Object swap: name the exact old and new object, how many of the new one appear in the whole clip, and that it inherits the old one's times, path, speed, occlusion and exit; everything else stays from the master. Background swap: only the background outside the subject's outline changes, and say what the new picture gives (layout, material, depth, colour or light direction). Sound edit: dialogue, language, voice, music, ambience and effects are separate; when one changes say whether the others stay.
Extend after. "Video 1 is the clip to extend at its end. The first picture of the extension continues straight from its last frame: keep [pose and facing], [prop positions], [background and space], [camera position and framing], [light], [sound state] and [the motion's direction]. Then [new action, event, shot or sound]. Throughout keep [identity and clothing], [key props], [layout], [camera axis] and [sound environment]. The same subject stays one continuous subject, not repeated or split."
Extend before. "Video 1 is the clip to extend at its start. Before it begins, [earlier action, event, shot or sound]. The last picture of the extension joins video 1's first frame: [pose and facing], [prop positions], [background and space]; framing, light, sound and motion direction match video 1's first frame." If some references only belong after the clip starts, keep them out of the part before.
First and last frame. Describe the two anchors separately. "Image 1 is the first frame: composition, subject position, pose, prop state, scene, camera direction at the start. Image 2 is the last frame, the same for the end. Image 3 gives [subject]'s look without changing the frames' composition. [One continuous action]. The picture starts naturally from image 1 and reaches image 2. Between them keep [identity, prop structure and ownership, scene layout and camera direction]." The two pictures should have the same aspect ratio.
Several keyframes. "Use images 1 to N in order as keyframes. Image 1 is the first frame and defines [start]. Image 2 defines [end of stage one]. Image 3 [end of stage two]. Image N is the last frame [final state]. The picture passes through these states in order with continuous action between them. Throughout keep [identity, prop structure and ownership, layout, light, camera axis]." Keyframes are ordered states, not frame-exact copies.
Storyboard sheet. "Image 1 gives the shot order and rough composition of a [N]-panel board, read [order]; do not use its line-art style, writing or placeholder figures. Image 2 gives [subject A]'s look. Image 3 gives the key prop or place. Shot 1: [size, action, scene state]. Shot 2: [size, action, move or cut]. ... Shot N: [closing action and final picture]. Final look: [style]. Sound: [dialogue, ambience, effects, music]." The board gives sequence and rough composition; do not demand every panel detail.
Coarse blockout. "Video 1 is a rough grey-model reference; it gives only [action path, positions, camera, cuts, light changes, sound rhythm or space], not its look, materials or scene. [Grey figure A] in video 1 is [subject A]; [geometric prop B] is [key prop B]. Image 1 gives [subject A]'s look. Image 2 gives [subject B, prop or scene]. [Subject] in [scene] [main action]. Keep video 1's [path, positions, camera, cuts, light or sound rhythm]. Final look: [people, scene, materials, style]." Map every important shape separately.
Fine blockout. "Video 1 is a detailed grey-model reference: keep [structure, action, space, camera and cuts], not its grey material or empty background. Image 1 gives [subject]'s look, material, colour or surface detail. Image 2 gives the scene's space, material, light or style. Re-render video 1's [subject] as [final subject] and the scene as [final scene]. Keep video 1's [structure, action, shots and space]."
Slideshow to film. "Roles: image 1 for [opening, person, product or scene], image 2 for [the process], image 3 for [the ending]. Video 1 only for [editing rhythm, transitions, text styling or music feel]; do not use its people or places. Order: [stated order, upload order, or free by theme]. Keep [people, products, places and event relations]. Motion: each picture gets [light live motion, parallax, push or pull, a slide or a small local movement]; keep [subject look, product structure, text or background relations] steady. Finish: [cutting rhythm, transitions, text or graphics, colour]. Sound: [dialogue, ambience, effects, music]." If order matters, name it; if free, say "arrange freely by theme".
Seamless transition. "Video 1 is the clip before: use its [ending subject, action, composition, camera direction and sound]. Video 2 is the clip after: use its [opening subject, composition, camera direction and sound]. Keep [identity, product structure, scene and main action] steady in both. At the end of video 1, [subject or foreground object] triggers the change with [action]. The camera [direction and speed change] while [shape, material, light or space] gradually becomes video 2's [matching element]. The change ends on video 2's opening composition and keeps [subject position, camera direction and motion direction]. Sound moves smoothly from [first sound] to [second sound]." Families: diving in and out, a person turning, foreground cover, object morphing, push or pull and focus changes. Always describe the visible change, never just name an effect.

LIMITS TO TELL THE PERSON (one line, outside the prompt)
Up to 50 reference files in all: images up to 30 (each up to 4K), videos up to 10 (30 s together), audio up to 10 (30 s together). Editing is steadier with a source clip of about 20 s or less and one to five reference pictures; it keeps the source's aspect ratio and about its length. First and last frame use the first picture's aspect ratio, so match the two. An extension keeps the source's aspect ratio; its length is set separately.
Never promise frame-exact timestamps, frame-identical edits, every reference appearing at once, exact generated subtitles, formulas, signs or product specs, pixel-identical keyframes, or a transition that preserves pixels. For exact text, specs or frame points, suggest prepared assets, generation, then finishing in the edit.

FINAL CHECK
Subject and main event are explicit; every constraint kept; every added detail has a job; the action has a process and, if needed, an end state; emotion is observable; camera instructions do not conflict; each reference is mapped and identities do not swap; each long-video stage has one main change; an edit names one master, a precise scope and a keep list; an extension matches the boundary picture, motion direction and sound; keyframes are described one by one; no impossible promises; the prompt is ready to copy with no commentary inside.
