MOTION FILM CRAFT

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading three motion-design skill packs the owner uploaded: a motion-direction method (Apache 2.0), a launch-film director (MIT) and a motion prompt writer (MIT). The originals are kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/motion-direction/. This is about planning and prompting short motion pieces, launch videos, product reels, explainers and loops. Use it when the person wants one of those; keep it quiet otherwise.

1. START WITH FOUR LINES
- Problem (what is this video fixing), Audience (who watches, where, in what state: a muted feed, a buyer on a call), Goal (what should they do after) and Emotion (where do they start and where should they end).
- Decide story first, then frames, then style, then words, then motion. Animating before the story is fixed is how pieces end up generic.
- Find the one true thing: one real feature, behaviour or moment. Specific beats general.

2. FIND AN IDEA THAT ONLY THIS BRAND COULD FILM
- Look for it in the logo's shape, the thing people touch, the material the product fills its screen with, the key verb, the success moment, the voice and the colours' meanings.
- Write the first idea, then at least two more of different kinds: a real task shown from start to result, one held shot that slowly changes, a small joke with a character, words as the picture, a montage of real output, calm that breaks and returns. Avoid defaulting to a chain of things turning into other things.
- Test each: cover the logo (does it still say who it is?) and swap in a competitor (would it still work for them?). If yes, it is the category's film, not an idea yet. Pick one and say why in a line.

3. EVERY BEAT BUYS ONE BELIEF
- For each beat write what the viewer comes to believe, or cut it. Roles: hook, turn, proof, call to action. Proof gets the largest share.
- Show it, then say it: a claim in words is followed by the product doing it.
- Shots hand off: the last frame of one is the first frame of the next. Cut on motion, not on rest. Things arrive by moving, not by fading in. Arrival slows down; exits speed up.
- Spend emphasis once: one clear subject per frame, at most a quarter of the beats are "the point", and the ending is quieter than the start. No frame is dead; a held frame keeps one living thing.
- End once, briefly. A closing logo-and-button card is held no longer than about a second and a half.

4. MATERIAL AND LOOK
- Use real material only. Never invent numbers, names, prices, logos or integrations; if data is only an example, say "Example data" on screen. Never redraw another product's interface.
- One world per piece: one logic of light, material, lens and drawing. Surfaces, not flat colour swatches.
- Words move at the speed of speech and are not typed letter by letter unless that is the product's own input.

5. NEVER SHIP
- Corner labels, counters, timecodes, progress bars, a name tag in the corner.
- Slide layouts: a kicker over a headline, a left headline with a device on the right.
- Generic card interfaces with a status dot, invented statistics, a stat wall.
- Slogan copy: runs of one-word sentences, "Not just X, it's Y", "Unlock", "the future of".
- A film nobody looked at: always look at stills and a contact sheet before calling it done.

6. WRITING THE PROMPT FOR A SHORT MOTION PIECE (eight parts)
1) The bar: the quality level in one line. 2) Format: size, exact seconds, seamless loop or not, music on or off. 3) The one rule: one hero object fills most of the frame and shows ONE satisfying change, then resets. 4) Look: background, depth (shadows, glass, highlights), one or two accent colours given as exact hex codes the person supplied, the font, the hero word large, labels never small, at most five words on screen. 5) Motion: natural spring motion with a small settle, nothing linear, hold the finished state for over a second, one thing always alive but only one focus. 6) Never: placeholder bars, stock icons, emoji, clip art, other brands' logos. 7) Check: look at a still every second, then again small, and fix anything generic or empty. 8) The scene: one moment in two or three sentences.
- Never guess a colour code; ask. If essential facts are missing (the moment, the exact words and numbers, the size), ask once in a batch of up to four short questions.
- With no brand given, say you are using a clean light default and offer to replace it.
- In Eclipse the picture is made by a video model: keep the prompt to one moment and one camera move, and use the wording rules from Cinema Craft and the Seedance notes when they apply.

7. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Atlas)
- Give the one idea and the beats in a few lines, then the prompt, instead of a lecture. Ask for missing real words and numbers rather than inventing them.
