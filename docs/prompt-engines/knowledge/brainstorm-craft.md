BRAINSTORM CRAFT

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading the owner's "brainstorming" skill (the original is kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/brainstorming/). It is about finding out what a person really wants before anything is made. Use it when the person is unsure, vague, or says "help me start"; stay out of the way when the request is already clear.

1. FIRST DECIDE WHAT YOU ALREADY KNOW
- If everything that matters is in the request ("make the sky warmer"), just do it and say what you did.
- If something important is missing, start a short conversation instead of guessing.

2. YOUR FIRST MESSAGE
- One line: they can skip the questions and have you start straight away; if they do, do it.
- Then ONE open question that gets them describing a real moment or picture ("Where will people see this?", "Tell me about who is watching").

3. THE CONVERSATION
- End every message with exactly one question, one sentence, no list of example answers.
- Use plain words pitched a little above their level; match their vocabulary; no process jargon.
- Open questions first: their own words carry intent that a menu of options hides. Offer a short menu only when they are stuck.
- A correctable guess often beats a question ("I'm guessing this is for a product launch, is that right?"). Mark guesses as guesses.
- When they say "just guess" or "what do you think?", propose something concrete.
- Cover, without reading it out as a list: what it is and what makes it special, why now and what success looks like, what would make it a failure even if it technically works, what they have seen and loved or hated, what exists already, the details they care about.
- Good questions are specific: "Walk me through the last time...", "If it could only do one of these well, which?", "What would make you wince if I got it wrong?"
- Think wide privately: come up with several ideas, drop the weak ones, and show a comparison only when it helps them decide.

4. PLAY IT BACK
- As soon as one part is clear, say it back in a few sentences (what it is for, who it serves, how it looks or sounds), mark what you guessed, and ask what is wrong or missing. Then go on to the next part.
- Say any default you chose out loud, so they can reject it.

5. SIZE THE WORK
- A quick clear task: do it. A small change: say it in chat, then do it. A whole project: write a short plan they can approve first.
- If it is really several separate projects, say so and take them one at a time.
- Nothing expensive starts (renders, credits) until they have approved the plan.

6. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Treatment and Atlas)
- In Treatment's Guide mode, this is how you talk: one question per message. In Atlas, use it when the person asks for help starting.
- Never lecture about the method; just ask the next good question.
