PRODUCTION PACK CRAFT

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading the owner's "filmmaking-producer" skill (the original is kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/filmmaking-producer/). It turns loose film notes into a plan a crew, or a series of generations, can follow. Use it when the person asks for a logline, treatment, beat sheet, shot list, schedule, budget or pitch.

1. INTAKE
- Find out the format (short film, ad, music video, social cut) and what they need out of it (a pitch, a polished script, a schedule, a shoot pack).
- If the notes are messy, sort them into premise, conflict, stakes, tone and references first.

2. THE STORY PACKAGE
- A logline in one line: who must do what before when, or what is lost.
- A few title options, a one-paragraph synopsis and a beat sheet: setup, inciting incident, rising conflict, midpoint, crisis, climax, resolution.

3. THE PRODUCTION PACKAGE
- Scene breakdown: for each scene its number, interior or exterior, place, time of day, characters, what happens, and any props, effects or sound it needs.
- Shot list: for each shot the scene, shot size, lens or movement, the purpose of the shot and notes. In Eclipse each shot is one generation, so keep one clear action per shot.
- Lists of props, wardrobe and locations. State the limits you assumed: budget, cast size, number of days, equipment, or credits.

4. BUDGET AND SCHEDULE
- A rough budget as ranges by line item, never as exact figures. For Eclipse work, translate it into the number of generations and the credits they are likely to use, and say the prices come from the live price list.
- A one-day plan and a multi-day plan.

5. THE PITCH PAGE
- A hook, who it is for, comparable titles, and how it will be made.

6. HONESTY RULES
- Label every assumption as an assumption.
- Never state legal or contract terms as fact; mark them as draft wording.
- Never say permits, rights or licences are settled unless the person told you so.

7. END EVERY PACK WITH
- The notes you used, what you produced, the open risks, and the decisions you need from the person.

8. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Treatment and Atlas)
- Give the smallest pack that answers the request; offer the next piece rather than producing all of them.
