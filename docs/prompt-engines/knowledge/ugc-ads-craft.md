UGC AD CRAFT

Eclipse notes in our own words, written after reading two UGC skill packs the owner uploaded (an ads strategy guide and an AI UGC production loop); the originals are kept unchanged in docs/prompt-engines/knowledge/original/ugc-ads/. Nothing in them about outside tools, creator marketplaces, prices or the route that works around a provider's refusals is used. This is for planning and writing UGC-style ads and the clips that make them. Use it when the person wants creator-style or "UGC" ads, hook variations or an ad test plan.

1. START WITH SIX ANSWERS (ask only what is missing)
What is promoted; the budget (including none); the platforms; whether real creators are in play or only AI; the one goal (awareness, sales or both); and what exists already (a phone recording of the product, a presenter they like, a voice reference).

2. THE FLOW, WITH THREE STOPS WHERE THE PERSON CHOOSES
- Find the pattern: look at what already works in the niche (the first spoken line in the first three seconds, when the product is first named, who is on camera) and say what pattern dominates. Tell the person what you based it on; do not invent statistics. STOP: they pick the presenter vibe and the voice.
- Build the character once: pin every detail an image model would otherwise fill in by default (skin texture and imperfections, eyes, teeth, hair, clothes, phone-camera softness, the room), in one locked description, and make the same description on two models so they can compare. STOP: they pick the character.
- Write several hooks, then make one clip per hook. STOP: they pick which hooks to make. Rerun one thing at a time; a rerun can break something else.
- Judge voice likeness and lip sync by eye and ear; say you cannot verify them from a transcript.

3. AD SHAPES (all vertical; the hook lands in the first three seconds or the viewer scrolls)
- Problem and fix: the daily pain, make it worse, the product as the fix, a result, one call to action. Cold traffic.
- Testimonial: "I was sceptical but...", the before, how they found it, a specific result, a direct recommendation. Trust.
- Unboxing: arrival, honest reaction, first use, a plain verdict, "would you try it?". Launches.
- Tutorial: "here is how I [outcome]", three or four steps with the product, the finished result, "save this". Retargeting.
- Comparison: "stop doing [old way]", the frustrating old way, the product doing it better, side by side. Competitive.
- Day in the life: the product folded into a routine, one genuine moment, how the day improved.
- Reaction: react to a headline, clip or statistic, add context, tie it to the product, prove it.
- Shop style: product first, demo, why it is worth the price, honest urgency (only if true).
For 15 to 30 seconds keep three beats: hook, the core moment, a kicker with the call to action.

4. A BRIEF THAT ANY CREATOR (OR A GENERATION) CAN FOLLOW
Brand and product with its one or two key facts; platform and format; length; vertical; two alternative hooks; two or three talking points (a pain or benefit, proof, one difference); the call to action; the look (natural light, product in use, at least one close-up, no studio look); and what to avoid (reading from a script, brand jargon, competitor names).

5. AI OR HUMAN
AI is good for testing many hooks, many languages, speed and volume. Real people are better when the product needs a physical demo, when authenticity is the brand, or when the ad needs a real account behind it. A good mix: test hooks and scripts with AI, then have real creators film the winners. Be open about what is AI-made where the platform or the law asks for it, and never use a real person's face or voice without their permission.

6. TEST IN A GRID, ONE VARIABLE AT A TIME
Three hooks (a pain, a result, a curiosity) x three bodies (a story, a list, a demo) x three calls to action (direct, "comment for the link", "tag someone"). Test hooks first with the same body and ending, then the body, then the ending, then combine the winners. Judge hooks on how many people stay past the first seconds, bodies on how long they keep watching and clicks, endings on the cost per result.
Stop what is clearly failing early, add budget to what holds up, and replace creatives before they tire (falling click rate, rising cost, high frequency, sour comments). Treat any number you quote as a typical starting point to compare with the person's own results, not a promise.

7. YOUR DELIVERY RULES (Atlas)
- Give one format and its beats, then the hook options, before any strategy. Keep each clip prompt to one moment, in the style of the Seedance and Cinema notes when they apply.
