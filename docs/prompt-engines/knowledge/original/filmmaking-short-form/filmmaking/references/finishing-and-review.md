# Finishing and review

Use for accessible versions, final exports, critiques, and revisions. Scale evidence to the work's actual consequences. A concept draft does not need a delivery dossier; a final master needs more than an export-success message.

## Make the experience accessible

Identify the information carried by speech, meaningful sound, and visual-only action. Provide accurate captions, a transcript, description, or other appropriate access according to the work and destination. Sound may carry essential meaning; accessibility need not force that meaning into decorative onscreen copy in every master.

When silent autoplay or silent viewing is part of the brief, author and inspect that experience explicitly, including captions and necessary visual context. A sound-led film can also have an accessible version without its original being required to convey the identical emotional experience on mute. A silent inspection remains useful for visual clarity, but is not a universal creative pass/fail.

For captions:

- preserve wording, speaker distinctions when needed, and meaningful non-speech information;
- phrase cues for comprehension, avoiding accidental word or idea breaks;
- give cues sufficient reading time at the intended size and pace;
- describe audible events without inventing emotion, intention, or physiology;
- inspect how a cue continues, clears, or repositions at a new shot, subject, or proof point;
- protect faces, action, names, graphics, and destination controls from avoidable collision.

Caption format, line count, styling, and overlap behavior should fit the actual player and applicable requirements. Inspect burned-in captions in the encoded picture and sidecars in the real player; caption-free video frames cannot prove sidecar placement. Do not automatically clear a caption merely because the picture cuts while its speech continues.

When visual-only meaning needs description, integrate it without trampling important dialogue or sound. Review the described experience, not just the existence of a text file. For web motion, flashing, autoplay controls, and reduced-motion alternatives, verify the applicable current accessibility requirements rather than treating a single numeric shortcut as compliance.

## Establish delivery truth

Confirm the requested versions and their destination specifications: canvas, frame rate, duration if constrained, codec, color, audio configuration, captions, and any required masters or stems. Verify volatile platform placement or specification details when they matter. Do not treat an old safe-area diagram as proof of the current interface.

Preserve a useful editable project and its required media or relink information when handoff or future revision calls for them. Identify the selected master and companion files unambiguously. Keep enough source, usage, version, and review information to distinguish the delivered work from superseded candidates. Do not add elaborate manifests where simple reliable identification suffices.

## Review the actual work

Watch and listen through the complete candidate in its intended form. Evaluate story and performance in continuous playback before isolated frame fixes. Then inspect consequential boundaries and dense states closely:

- opening orientation, significant turns, reactions, demonstrations, and ending;
- cut continuity, intentional discontinuity, sync, action completion, and spoken exits;
- text readability, caption timing and placement, moving crops, masks, and composite edges;
- audible seams, masking, pumping, missing tails, room-tone changes, and translation;
- shot matching, source interpretation, unintended clipping, and encode artifacts.

Use frame stepping, waveform, scopes, and representative image strips to investigate defects. They supplement motion and listening, not replace them. Distinguish intended black, stillness, silence, or a hard cut from an accidental technical fault.

Verify that the file decodes, carries the expected streams, has correct timing and sync, and meets the selected delivery settings. Measure decoded audio rather than trusting a source WAV. Inspect the intended viewing size and relevant speakers or headphones; actual destination playback matters when overlays, caption rendering, or room conditions could change the result.

Keep evidence categories honest. Software can measure timing, decode, levels, and file identity. It cannot approve dignity, natural performance, emotional settlement, or taste. State unperformed checks directly. Changed media needs the affected verification again; preserve prior review as history rather than implying it approved a different version.

## Revise by cause

Begin with the user's note and playback context. Identify the strongest quality to preserve and the likely cause of the problem. For example:

| Observation | Investigate before adding polish |
| --- | --- |
| A moment feels rushed | Recognition time, speech and gesture completion, breathing room, competing arrivals |
| Footage feels generic | Selection of an actual decision or action, perspective, sequence purpose |
| Graphics feel pasted on | Timing, alignment, motion, occlusion, or whether the graphics are needed |
| Sound feels like punctuation | Continuity, phrase, causal motivation, transient hierarchy, space |
| Voice sounds artificial | Casting, connected phrasing, emphasis, restoration artifacts, acoustic context |
| A sequence feels flat | Development, contrast, performance, stakes, or a missing shift in understanding |

These are hypotheses, not automatic diagnoses. When uncertain, compare a small number of alternatives while holding unrelated elements stable. Listen at comparable levels. Choose the revision that resolves the note and preserves the film, not the variant with the most changes.

For consequential work, a fresh viewer can reveal whether the piece communicates without an explanation. Distinguish confusion from a deliberate ambiguity that suits the brief. Conclude the critique with the strongest quality, the most consequential issue, and a specific next change when one is needed. Stop iterating when the requested outcome is met or the remaining decision is an identifiable creative choice rather than a demonstrable defect.

## Deliver clearly

Provide the requested media or draft, required companion material, current review status, and material limitations. Distinguish technical completion from creative acceptance and publication authority. Preserve approved work and supplied sources; cleanup or publication does not follow automatically from finishing an edit.
