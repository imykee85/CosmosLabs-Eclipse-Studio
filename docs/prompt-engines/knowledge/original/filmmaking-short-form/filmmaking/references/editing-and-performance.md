# Editing and performance

Use when selecting and arranging moving footage, performances, or dialogue. Let the scene's dramatic, informational, or observational purpose govern the edit.

## Find the action inside the source

Review the material around candidate moments. Identify orientation, the action or thought developing, its peak, the visible or spoken completion, and the aftermath. “Person working” is a category; a choice, failed attempt, handoff, discovery, or changed reaction is an editorial event.

Build selects around what the material actually supplies. Keep enough source handles to reconsider timing and transitions. Know whether an apparent reaction belongs to the same event and whether a juxtaposition invents a relationship. A transcript helps locate words but does not contain gesture, hesitation, overlap, irony, or room tone.

For a dialogue scene, listen to the whole exchange before trimming individual lines. Determine whose experience governs the scene and when that perspective shifts. The speaking face is not always the most informative picture; a listener's reaction, a hand, or held space may reveal subtext. Preserve intentional overlaps, interrupted thoughts, and silences when they are the action.

## Make the cut earn its place

For an uncertain cut, name its purpose, trigger, next focal target, and relationship to the preceding shot. Useful motivations include a completed or interrupted action, eyeline, revelation, verbal turn, matched movement, association, sonic prelap, or deliberate rupture. “More energy” should resolve into an observable change in rhythm or attention.

Use continuity when it serves the scene: screen direction, eyelines, movement, position, sound perspective, and matching action can keep attention on the subject. Break continuity deliberately when discontinuity supplies the desired experience. Do not apply a fixed transition menu to unrelated films; a cut, dissolve, fade, overlap, or persistent movement each creates a different relation in context.

Check perceptual continuity separately from event comprehension. First inspect where attention is likely to be just before the cut and where the next essential detail appears; if an unintended search interrupts the moment, try different timing, shot selection, or incoming composition. Then check whether the action and relationship remain understandable. A smooth match can conceal a missing operation or imply false causality; an intentional discontinuity can remain intelligible. This two-part check is a craft synthesis informed by [Smith's attentional theory](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/) and [Magliano and Zacks's event-segmentation study](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.wustl.edu/dist/e/952/files/2017/09/maglianoandzacks2011-22vhbrv.pdf). The former is an explanatory account and the latter examines one film; neither establishes a universal cutting rule or decides when disruption is artistically right.

Pace by recognition, anticipation, action, and inference. Evenly spaced cuts can erase important differences. Try holding the relevant shot longer before adding another angle. Conversely, a completion that the audience can infer may be shortened when that ellipsis is truthful and intelligible.

## Protect a thought without forbidding interruption

When a complete statement or demonstration is essential, check the later of required speech completion and visible completion, then allow enough release for the meaning to land. Do not start that release while a word, gesture, or necessary graphic is still unfinished. Natural performance may need a breath, a laugh, an eye movement, or a pause after the sentence.

This is a diagnostic for accidental truncation, not a universal ban on cutting mid-action or mid-sentence. Dramatic interruption, overlapping dialogue, comedy, urgency, and ellipsis may require an early exit. Identify its purpose and inspect the result for lost consonants, changed meaning, continuity damage, and unintended emotional coercion.

Listen across every consequential dialogue exit in the final sequence. Low waveform energy is not proof that the thought is over. A technically complete word can still sound cut off; a picture change can prematurely end an emotion even while audio continues.

## Split picture and sound intentionally

Use incoming sound to prepare the next space or thought; carry outgoing sound to preserve connection, reaction, or consequence. Listen for perspective and room-tone continuity. A smooth crossfade can still conceal a false relationship or an unnatural acoustic change.

Where a dialogue edit requires fill, use appropriate authentic room tone with compatible perspective. Avoid a short repeated texture that becomes audible as a loop. Do not replace meaningful ambience with digital silence. Further mix and voice decisions belong in [Sound, music, and voice](sound-music-and-voice.md).

## Retime for the visible event

Keep ordinary speed when physical causality, speech, procedure, or performance depends on it. Slow motion can give an action reflective or sensory weight; time compression can reveal accumulation or create contrast. Choose per moment, not as a global style applied to unrelated footage.

Inspect cadence, motion blur, interpolation artifacts, lips, source audio, and action completion. Preserve pitch when appropriate, but audition the treatment: pitch preservation does not guarantee a natural voice. Do not imply an action happened at a documentary speed that it did not when that changes the claim.

## Maintain a usable edit

Use the actual timeline or edit record as the authoritative timing source. Preserve source identity, in/out points, program placement, frame-rate interpretation, retiming, significant transforms, and audio decisions sufficiently for revision or conform. Avoid maintaining a second hand-written timing list that can silently diverge from the edit.

Reframe for each requested destination with the subject and meaningful action intact. An approved crop can be simple; it still needs deliberate inspection. When the action cannot survive a ratio, change the shot or layout, or discuss the required version rather than mechanically center-cropping. Do not create extra ratios absent a delivery need.
