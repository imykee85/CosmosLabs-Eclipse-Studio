# Motion and composition

Use for graphics, animation, typography, compositing, and alternate framing. Preserve an established visual identity when the project has one; otherwise derive a visual language from the film's subject and intention.

## Compose the attention hierarchy

Know what the viewer should notice first and how attention moves. Scale, contrast, negative space, sharpness, motion onset, and sound can all direct the eye. Reserve their strongest combination for what matters. A clear composition may need no supporting graphics.

Treat typography as timed information. Measure actual rendered type, line spacing, and surrounding space at the intended playback size. A nominal bounding box does not prove that glyphs fit or that adjacent lines read cleanly. Prefer sequencing or reducing dense text before reducing it to an unreadable size. Give readable states adequate time after their entrances finish.

Use appropriate contrast and separation across the underlying moving image. A plate, shadow, different position, or a clean picture interval can help; no single palette or plate opacity is required. Captions have their own accessibility obligations and should not compete with designed labels or cover meaningful action.

## Author motion from poses and relationships

Design the meaningful states first, then their transitions. Anticipation, action, and settle can communicate direction and weight, but not every move needs overshoot or anticipation. Separate the spatial path from its timing curve so revising speed does not accidentally change its geometry.

Distinguish primary motion, supporting motion, and ambience. Offset supporting peaks when simultaneous changes compete for attention. Useful restrained starting points include a small spatial overshoot or a few frames of secondary lag; use them only when they describe the material or action. Avoid unintended oscillation between identical keyframe values. Opacity and color transitions should remain within their intended range.

Motion should finish in a state that makes sense. Inspect intermediate frames, not just attractive endpoints: tangencies, crop leaks, disconnected lines, bad easing, and one-frame exposure can appear only during travel. Judge thin high-contrast detail for judder at the actual frame rate. Shorten travel, slow the move, simplify detail, or use suitable motion blur or a different production frame rate according to the brief. Do not assume a post-conversion recreates the temporal information of a native capture or render.

Ambient movement is optional. Rings might breathe, texture might drift, and depth layers might separate slightly when the image benefits. Keep text and the focal read stable where reading is the purpose. Stop ambient activity when it competes with the performance or ending; do not animate a held image merely to prove it is a video.

## Make picture and graphics share a world

Allow footage to establish its action before placing demands on it. A graphic can inherit an axis, movement direction, contact, reveal, occlusion, or meaningful color from the shot. Use it to add necessary information or a deliberate visual relationship. Remove it when the picture already carries the intended inference.

Build related plate, border, accent, and content as a coherent component. Their geometry, transform, and visibility should stay related through the shot. A caption accent should not linger after its plate or intersect its text. Optical balance still needs picture review even when numerical insets match.

Classify a line by its role: accent, divider, connector, route, or measurement. Keep endpoints and crossings meaningful. A route crossing an object needs a consistent relationship in depth; a line that explains nothing may be unnecessary. Use masks and occlusion deliberately rather than allowing accidental depth flips.

For layered work, establish depth, pivot, overlap, padding, shadow, and alpha behavior before making assets. Preserve straight versus premultiplied alpha through interchange; inspect edges over light and dark backgrounds as well as checkerboard. A silhouette extracted from pixels is not automatically an editable contour suitable for path animation. Use artwork whose structure supports the intended deformation or motion.

Generated imagery may supply fictional worlds, illustration, material, or design when the brief permits it. Keep required lettering, marks, and exact geometry under reliable authoring control and inspect the result. Preserve source and derivative identity enough to revise the work. Do not let generated material silently become documentary proof.

## Compose the requested versions

Distinguish the encoded frame, meaningful action area, typography area, and destination interface overlays. Inspect essential subjects and actions throughout their paths. A point inside the frame does not prove a face, hand, or demonstration survives the crop well.

Reconsider framing, text amount, shot choice, and timing for each requested ratio. A single source moment can have different emphasis across versions while preserving its meaning. Simple reframing is valid when it works; automatic center-cropping without review is not sufficient evidence.

Exact brand colors, marks, fonts, and final-card rules belong to the project's identity and delivery requirements. A final mark may require time to read, but neither a mark nor a fixed final hold belongs in every film.
