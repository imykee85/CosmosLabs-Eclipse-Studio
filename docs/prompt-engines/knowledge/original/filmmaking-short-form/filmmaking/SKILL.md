# Filmmaking

Make the viewer's experience the organizing decision: what they notice, anticipate, understand, and feel, and how that changes over time. Let real performance, image, sound, and silence contribute meaning. Use the user's established tool stack in service of the creative work.

## Establish the job

Identify the form, audience, intended experience, central question or proposition, source material, approximate scope, and viewing context. Establish whether the request is for a concept, script, edit, finishing pass, critique, or deliverable. Preserve the user's chosen form and existing strong work. A short companion to a longer script is a distinct work, not a complete adaptation.

Resolve consequential unknowns; make low-risk choices and proceed where they do not affect the answer. A missing delivery specification need not prevent writing or source analysis. Do not invent testimony, source clearance, footage, completed production, or viewer reactions.

For a new piece, form a compact working brief in the project's natural format. Capture the audience's starting point; the question, tension, or experience; the material that can carry it; the desired change or deliberate unresolved state; and relevant representation and delivery constraints. This is a decision aid, not a mandatory schema.

## Shape and execute the piece

Give scenes and sequences a purpose. A cut or juxtaposition can change information, perspective, emotion, rhythm, or association; an image need not explain itself in copy. Choose continuity, contrast, repetition, interruption, and duration for the intended effect. Leave room for observation and ambiguity where the form benefits from them.

Treat picture and sound as interacting decisions from the beginning. Select the performance and meaningful action before decorating them. In music-led work, the musical structure may lead picture; in testimony, the speaker may determine pace. Read the sound reference before choosing a music bed, not only when polishing the mix.

Reuse the user's recorded tool stack and working conventions across projects and tasks. If no choice is recorded, ask during initial setup about their available tools and preferred editing stack, or present currently verified available options and let them choose. Do not infer a preference from installed tools. Once the user explicitly chooses, record that choice and its scope in the appropriate durable user or project preference location, following its write rules; retain cross-project preferences at user scope and project-specific exceptions with the project. Do not invent a choice or write memory merely to initialize this workflow.

Keep the chosen stack rather than selecting a new toolset for each task. Revisit it only when the user requests a change, a chosen tool becomes unavailable, or a concrete capability or compatibility gap merits discussing an addition or change. Verify needed capabilities within the established stack without a mandatory full re-evaluation. Preserve editable decisions and useful source handles, and do not force footage, dialogue, or music into an unsuitable template. Verify current official documentation when recommending a specific product or feature. Openness to new tools does not authorize installation; installation, spending, publication, and source alteration retain their existing authorization boundaries.

Keep source identity and usage context sufficient to support the work. Distinguish documentary evidence, performance, reenactment, illustration, and generated material. Generated material can serve a declared fictional or visual role; it must not masquerade as recorded actuality. Protect the represented person's agency and the truth of testimony through selection, juxtaposition, sound, and grading.

## Finish according to the brief

Judge the finished sequence in motion and with its intended sound, then inspect details that could change the experience. Numerical settings in the references are starting points or diagnostics unless the destination or brief makes them requirements. No universal duration, final card, palette, single emotional crest, musical meter, or silent-viewing rule applies to every film.

For a revision, identify the note's cause, preserve what works, and change the relevant system. Use a controlled comparison when it will settle a real uncertainty. Keep creative judgment, technical verification, required listening or accessibility checks, and final acceptance distinguishable. Report what was actually reviewed and what remains unresolved; a successful export is not creative acceptance.

Deliver the requested work in a useful form, with the chosen master or draft clearly identified, required companion files, and a concise statement of material limitations. Use [Finishing and review](references/finishing-and-review.md) for the detailed checks.
