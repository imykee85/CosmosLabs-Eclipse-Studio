# Provenance and limits

Version 2.0 — 30 October 2026.

This revision incorporates still/first-frame/project-record capabilities from the user's installed Cinematic Seedance adaptation, whose stated influences were user-supplied Scenecraft v1 and Shotcaller v1. The new guidance and examples are independently written; original source documents are not bundled. It also analyzes the user-supplied Scene 109 station/escalator/boarding prompt as text. No corresponding reference assets or rendered clip were supplied for that audit, and no generation was submitted.

The additions make actor intention, attention, physical effort and recovery explicit; distinguish source/material appearance from motion and delivery failures; add scene-plate workflows and optional project/shot/take records; and strengthen timing and contradiction checks. Acting and cinematography sources explain the adopted concepts, not proof of Seedance compliance. See [performance direction](performance-direction.md) and [visual realism](visual-realism.md).

Package validation and independent prompt-only exercises do not establish a reduction in plasticity, human-quality acting, or a generation success rate. Such claims require inspected outputs and repeated comparisons. The following original research record is retained from version 1.0; its 20-project-prompt survey was not repeated during this revision.

## Original study record — 29 October 2026

This independent skill synthesizes patterns observed in **20 distinct, fully expanded production prompts** visible in the public [Cully Hill Boys project on Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/generate?projectId=b619c13c-83ba-4ea3-b85c-de9be41bd01b&from=%2Foriginal-series%2Fcully-hill-boys%2Ffull-film). Each counted prompt was opened with **See all** and the expanded **Hide** control verified. Six samples came from Act 1, seven from Act 2, and seven from the epilogue/root view. One incomplete additional source was excluded.

The displayed metadata labeled **19 samples Seedance 2** and **one Seedance 2.5**. Five of the Seedance 2 samples displayed 4k and 4032×1728; fourteen displayed 1080p and 2016×864. The later 2.5 sample displayed 720p alongside 2016×864; those inconsistent UI labels were recorded, not reconciled by assumption. The sample is not described as exclusively Seedance 2 or as hundreds of prompts.

Full prompt text was analyzed. Representative playing-preview frames were inspected for several samples; the study did not watch every clip end to end, inspect original encoded media, validate all dialogue/audio, or run controlled A/B generations. These are visible production prompts attributed to creators in a shared project—not a hidden Higgsfield system prompt, proof of an optimal formula, or evidence of one model's internal mechanism.

Reusable observations include limited reference responsibilities, first-frame geography, timed action, camera motivation, physical contact, staged reactions, source-based lighting, speaker attribution and continuity priorities. The skill deliberately corrects observed contradictions, repetitive locks, implausible timing and unsupported technical adjectives. It also preserves intentional cold light, spherical optics, still cameras or clean texture when the brief calls for them.
